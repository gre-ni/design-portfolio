During my years at Wrike, I have created many presentation templates and design. While absolute majority is of course confidential, here are a few slides taken from a public annual event Collaborate.

A couple of things I focus on when designing for decks:

- Templates need to be highly versatile and provide both informative and engaging options. Real use lives life of its own, people will create local copies and change things and so the design needs to preempt the needs of stakeholders as much as possible.
- The audience sees each slide for barely a second, good structure, hierarchy and highlighting is key.
- It is just as important to think about the speaker as it is to think about the audience. The slide deck gives them confidence, helps them communicate a story, and drive home a message.
- When creating diagrams and infographic, it is ten times better to ask for a "bad sketch" in MS Paint than trying to guess the layout from the description or information alone.
- Provide as many icons and illustrations as possible. If people don't have what they need, they'll reach outside of branded asets.
