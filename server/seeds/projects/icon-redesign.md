The icon redesign project was one of my self-directed brand improvement initiatives at Wrike, contributing to the push of making our brand and overall design system feel more cohesive, consistent and up to date.

Over the previous 3 years, the brand identity of Wrike has undergone a lot of changes, and so the previous icon set was one of the artefacts of an older design system. Before, the brand could be characterised by filled shapes, a rather illustrative style and a more extended colour palette.

Today, the branding is much simpler, relying on minimal geometric shapes and lines, which I wanted to see reflected in our iconography.

The biggest challenge for this icon redesign was that this particular size, 64x64, sits somewhere between a UI utility icon and a spot illustration. Since the icons already existed within an ecosystem of hundreds of pages, the jump couldn't be too big. It has been no easy feat to maintain the balance between staying a mostly minimal geometric brand feel and also communicating enough information and keeping the same level of engagement.

I focused the most on making mindful decisions between abstraction and readability. The icons need to look nice but also preserve their clarity. It is also important to keep in check an overall level of complexity and keep that as consistent as possible - a simple heart shape needs to have the same visual weight as an icon symbolising a whole industry or a software feature.

Finally, my goal here wasn't to just produce a set of icons but to set up a new system. I provided a grid, rules for corners and line weight, but also a set of further guidelines to help future designers in understanding and expanding the set accurately.
